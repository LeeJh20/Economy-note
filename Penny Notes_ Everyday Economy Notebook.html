Prices and inflation

Why $10 buys less than it used to.

Read the note

Inflation is a broad, sustained rise in prices. When it happens, each unit of money buys a little less.

Money flows

Interest rates

If money had a price, this would be it.

Read the note

An interest rate is the price of borrowing money, and the reward for lending it. Higher rates make loans costlier and saving more attractive.

Money flows

Exchange rates

Why a strong dollar makes trips abroad pricier.

Read the note

An exchange rate is how much of one currency you get for another. When your home currency weakens, foreign goods and travel cost more.

Daily life

Supply and demand

Buyers, sellers, and the price in between.

Read the note

When many people want something that is scarce, its price tends to rise. When supply floods in, prices tend to fall.

Big picture

GDP and growth

How big did a country's economy get?

Read the note

Gross domestic product adds up the value of goods and services produced in a country over a period. Growth is its change over time.

Daily life

Opportunity cost

What you give up when you choose.

Read the note

Every choice has a next-best alternative you skip. An evening at the cinema costs the ticket plus whatever else you could have done.

Inflation: why the same money buys less

A closer look at one idea.

Inflation means the general level of prices keeps rising. One pricey coffee is not inflation. Statisticians track a basket of typical purchases, such as food, transport and housing, in a consumer price index (CPI).

Prices can rise when demand outpaces what producers can supply, or when costs like energy and raw materials go up. Usually several causes overlap, and not every price moves equally.

Inflation rateA basket going from 100 to 102 is a 2% rise: (102 − 100) ÷ 100.
Purchasing powerAt 2% inflation, 100 buys about 98 worth of yesterday’s goods: 100 ÷ 1.02 ≈ 98.04.
Rate vs. levelWhen inflation slows from 5% to 2%, prices still rise, just more slowly. Falling prices overall is called deflation.
